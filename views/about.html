About BodaLink

Boda rides and parcels, with riders you can check.

BodaLink connects passengers and senders with verified boda riders in Kimana and Oloitokitok. You say where you are going, riders near you bid, and you pick the price.

Why we built it

Getting a boda should not mean waiting at the stage, guessing the price or not knowing who you are riding with. Sending a parcel should not mean hoping a stranger delivers it. BodaLink puts a clear price, a named rider and a record of the trip in one place.

How it works

  1. Tell us where to pick you up and where you are going. For a parcel, say what you are sending and who receives it.
  2. Riders online near you bid within seconds. You see each price, the rider's rating and how far away they are.
  3. You choose a rider and they come to you. You pay them directly. Parcels use a pickup code, so the right rider collects.

Who rides with BodaLink

Every rider is checked before their first job. They give their national ID number, a photo of the ID, a photo of the bike that shows the number plate, and a clear photo of their face. A person at BodaLink reviews these, usually within a day. Riders who give passengers a helmet can earn a Helmet verified badge once we have checked it.

Everyone adds a profile photo when they sign up, so riders and passengers can recognise each other. Passengers sign up with an email, a phone number and a photo. Adding an ID number and an emergency contact is optional, and a verified badge follows once we confirm the ID.

How riders earn

Riders set their own price on every job and keep all of it. BodaLink takes nothing from a fare. To go online, a rider buys a day pass for KSh 100, which covers 24 hours. The pass is paid with M-Pesa. New riders get their first day free once they are approved.

The app

BodaLink installs from your browser, so there is no app store and no large download. It is built to work on weak data. Bids and job alerts show up live, routes open in Google Maps, and you can share a trip with family or friends on WhatsApp.

Where we work

Kimana and Oloitokitok in Kajiado County, including trips toward the Amboseli National Park gates at Kimana, Meshanani and Iremito.
